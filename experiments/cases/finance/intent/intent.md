# Quarterly Finance Report Pipeline Requirements Document  

## Introduction  
This document outlines the requirements for establishing a pipeline that processes quarterly financial data into a comprehensive and accurate written report. The objective of this pipeline is to provide stakeholders with key financial insights based on revenue and expense data while adhering to regulatory standards. At its core, the pipeline must ensure the integrity of revenue classifications, the accuracy of tax audits, and the clarity of reporting. This document serves as a guiding handbook for all team members involved in the workflow, with clearly defined roles and expectations.  

## Background and Scope of Work  
The quarterly finance report is a crucial deliverable that supports decision-making and ensures financial compliance. Stakeholders rely on this report to evaluate business performance, spot trends, and coordinate with external auditors or regulators when necessary.  

The pipeline begins with raw revenue and expense data and ends with a comprehensive report. Along the way, revenue and expense data undergo meticulous classification and analysis. In particular, revenues exceeding $50,000 ("high revenue") are flagged for special attention due to their need for a tax audit. All revenue classifications, whether "high revenue" or "standard revenue," require explicit verification by a tax specialist or verifier before being included in the final report. Expense data is analyzed separately and reported alongside revenue analysis in the final written document.  

## Team Roles and General Responsibilities  
To streamline the workflow and promote accountability, specific roles are assigned to team members who handle different stages of the pipeline. Each role has a critical contribution toward the completion of the quarterly finance report. Below are the roles involved in the workflow:  

- **Fetcher**: Responsible for retrieving raw data. The Fetcher ensures access to the most accurate and up-to-date revenue and expense figures from relevant sources. The Fetcher also serves as the final recipient of the written report.  
- **Revenue Analyst**: Responsible for evaluating revenue data and classifying each revenue entry as "high revenue" (i.e., above $50,000) or "standard revenue" (i.e., $50,000 or less). The Revenue Analyst must produce substantive revenue analysis for every entry, meeting a minimum threshold of detail and clarity (more than 10 characters).  
- **Expense Analyst**: Focused on analyzing operating expense data. Expense analysis must also meet a minimum substantive threshold, showing more than 10 characters of detailed information for accuracy and context.  
- **Writer**: Handles the composition of the quarterly finance report, compiling approved data from Revenue Analysts, Expense Analysts, and Tax Verifiers. The Writer ensures the report is clear, professional, and thorough before delivering it back to the Fetcher.  
- **Tax Verifier**: Plays a crucial role in ensuring accuracy and compliance. The Tax Verifier explicitly approves every audit result submitted by Tax Specialists. Both high-revenue classifications and tax audits require explicit verification to proceed.  
- **Tax Specialist**: Responsible for auditing high-revenue entries (above $50,000) as requested. The Tax Specialist must produce a meaningful audit, without which the pipeline cannot proceed with approval and inclusion of the revenue in the final report.  

These roles must work collaboratively to maintain high standards of data integrity, tax compliance, and reporting excellence.  

## Detailed Requirements for Each Activity  

### 1. Raw Data Retrieval  
The Fetcher must ensure that revenue and expense data are obtained accurately and on time. Without high-quality raw data access, every downstream activity in the pipeline risks inaccuracies. The Fetcher must provide reliable data retrieval mechanisms and respond promptly to requests when other team members need clarification regarding the data source.

### 2. Revenue Analysis Requirements  
Revenue analysis conducted by the Revenue Analyst is pivotal to the pipeline. Each piece of revenue data must be classified appropriately:  

- **High Revenue (> $50,000)**: Any revenue entry exceeding $50,000 falls into this category and triggers additional workflow requirements for detailed tax audits. These entries must be forwarded to the Tax Specialist for auditing.  
- **Standard Revenue ($50,000 or less)**: Revenues falling below or equal to $50,000 are classified as standard revenue and do not require a tax audit but still require explicit verification by the Tax Verifier.  

Regardless of category, each revenue analysis must meet the following standard:  
- **Substantive analysis:** Descriptions and classifications provided by the Revenue Analyst must include at least 10 characters of meaningful detail to ensure clarity and accountability. This applies equally to high-revenue and standard-revenue classifications.  

### 3. Expense Analysis Requirements  
Operating expenses must be separately analyzed by the Expense Analyst as part of the pipeline. Similar to revenue analysis, expense analysis must meet a substantive threshold and provide meaningful detail exceeding 10 characters.  

Expense classifications and summaries should be clear and well-articulated, as they feed directly into the final report. The Expense Analyst must ensure that analyses highlight trends and support any overarching insights presented in the report.  

### 4. Tax Audit Process  
When revenue entries are classified as high revenue (above $50,000), the Tax Specialist is required to conduct an audit. This audit must produce meaningful findings that cannot be empty or superficial. The audit determines whether the numbers are accurate, compliant, and properly categorized.  

The Tax Verifier must approve audit findings before high-revenue entries are deemed eligible for inclusion in the final report. This explicit approval step ensures compliance with tax regulations and prevents discrepancies from undermining the overall reliability of the report.  

### 5. Report Compilation and Delivery  
The Writer plays the critical role of weaving all approved analyses into a cohesive quarterly report. The Writer receives input from Revenue Analysts, Expense Analysts, and Tax Verifiers and compiles the data in a manner that is both readable and actionable for stakeholders.  

Deliverables from this step include:  
- **Final Quarterly Finance Report:** A written document presenting revenue and expense insights, substantiated by analysis and approved audits. The report must terminate the pipeline and meet both substantive and professional standards.
- **Delivery of the report:** The finalized document must be delivered to the Fetcher, who ensures the stakeholder has the report available for review, archiving, or external submission.  

## Compliance Criteria  
The following criteria must be strictly adhered to for the pipeline to be considered successful:  
1. **Thresholds for revenue classification:** High revenue must be flagged correctly if it exceeds $50,000; entries below this amount must be classified as standard revenue.  
2. **Audit findings must be substantive and non-empty:** High-revenue audits cannot be left incomplete or devoid of meaningful data.  
3. **Verification of all classifications and audits:** The Tax Verifier must explicitly approve every audit finding before entries are included in the final report.  
4. **Minimum level of detail in analyses:** Revenue and expense analyses must exceed 10 characters of meaningful prose to ensure clarity and substance.  
5. **Completion and delivery of a final report:** The pipeline must produce a quarterly finance report as its end product, delivered to the Fetcher as the final step.  

Failure to meet any of these criteria will result in the pipeline being considered incomplete and the report unfit for delivery to stakeholders.  

## Expectations for Professional Standards  
The quarterly finance report is not merely an operational necessity but also a reflection of the organization’s professionalism and attention to compliance. As such, all team members are expected to:  
- Adhere to deadlines to ensure timely report delivery.  
- Maintain clear communication across roles to prevent misunderstandings or data inaccuracies.  
- Uphold ethical standards, particularly in high-revenue audits and tax verification.  
- Ensure that every component of the report, from raw data to written analysis, maintains clarity, correctness, and relevance.  

## Conclusion  
This requirements document outlines key components, roles, and expectations for the quarterly finance report pipeline. By meeting these specifications, your team will ensure consistency, accuracy, and compliance, producing a report that empowers stakeholders with reliable financial insights.
